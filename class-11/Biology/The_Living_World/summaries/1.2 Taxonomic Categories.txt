## 1.2 Taxonomic Categories
- Classification is not a single-step process but involves a **hierarchy of steps**, where each step represents a **rank** or **category**.
- Since a category is part of the overall taxonomic arrangement, it is called a **taxonomic category**. All categories together constitute the **taxonomic hierarchy**.
- Each category, referred to as a unit of classification, represents a rank and is commonly termed as **taxon** (plural: taxa).
- Insects, for example, represent a group with common features (three pairs of jointed legs) and can be classified, thus given a rank or category.
- Taxonomic groups/categories are distinct biological entities, not just morphological aggregates.
- Taxonomical studies have led to common categories such as **kingdom**, **phylum** or **division** (for plants), **class**, **order**, **family**, **genus**, and **species**.
- **Species** is the lowest category for all organisms, including plants and animals.
- Placing an organism in various categories requires knowledge of its individual or group characteristics, which helps identify similarities and dissimilarities.
