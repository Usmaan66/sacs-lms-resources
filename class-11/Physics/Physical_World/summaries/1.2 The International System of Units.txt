### 1.2 The International System of Units
- **Historical Systems of Units:**
    - In the past, different countries used different measurement systems.
    - Three common systems were:
        - **CGS system:** Centimetre (length), Gram (mass), Second (time).
        - **FPS (or British) system:** Foot (length), Pound (mass), Second (time).
        - **MKS system:** Metre (length), Kilogram (mass), Second (time).
- **The Modern International System (SI):**
    - The **Système Internationale d'Unités** (French for International System of Units), abbreviated as **SI**, is the globally accepted system for measurements.
    - It was developed by the Bureau International des Poids et Mesures (BIPM) in 1971 and recently revised in November 2018.
    - **Why SI?** SI units use the decimal system, which makes conversions within the system very simple and convenient.
    - SI is used worldwide in scientific, technical, industrial, and commercial work.
- **SI Base Quantities and Units:**
    - The SI system has **seven base units**:

| Base Quantity | Name | Symbol |
|---------------|------|--------|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric Current | ampere | A |
| Thermodynamic Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

    - *Note:* The detailed definitions of these units involve very precise physical constants and are given for information, not for memorization.
- **Supplementary Units (Plane Angle and Solid Angle):**
    - Besides the seven base units, there are two additional units:
        - **Plane Angle ($d\theta$):** Defined as the ratio of the arc length ($ds$) to the radius ($r$).
            - Formula: $d\theta = ds/r$
            - Unit: **radian** (symbol: **rad**)
            - *Diagram:* Imagine a slice of a circle. $ds$ is the curved edge, and $r$ is the straight side from the center to the edge. The angle is at the center (O).
        - **Solid Angle ($d\Omega$):** Defined as the ratio of the intercepted area ($dA$) on a spherical surface to the square of its radius ($r$).
            - Formula: $d\Omega = dA/r^2$
            - Unit: **steradian** (symbol: **sr**)
            - *Diagram:* Imagine a cone shape. $dA$ is the curved area at the wider end, and $r$ is the slant height from the apex (O) to the edge of $dA$.
    - Both plane angle and solid angle are **dimensionless quantities**.
- **Other Units for General Use (Outside SI):**
    - Some units are commonly used even though they are not part of the strict SI system. Examples include:
        - **minute (min):** 60 s
        - **hour (h):** 3600 s
        - **day (d):** 86400 s
        - **litre (L):** $10^{-3} m^3$
        - **tonne (t):** $10^3$ kg
        - **degree ($^\circ$):** $(\pi/180)$ rad
- **Important Note about Mole:** When using the mole unit, you must specify the elementary entities being referred to (e.g., atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, etc.).
- **SI Prefixes:** These are used to denote multiples and sub-multiples of units (e.g., kilo, milli, micro).

**Key Takeaway:** The SI system is the globally standardized system of units, built upon seven base units and two supplementary units (radian and steradian). It is decimal-based for easy conversions.
