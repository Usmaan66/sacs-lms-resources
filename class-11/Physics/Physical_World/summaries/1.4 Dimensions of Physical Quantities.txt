### 1.4 Dimensions of Physical Quantities
- **What are Dimensions?** The **dimensions** of a physical quantity describe its fundamental nature in terms of base quantities, independent of specific units.
- **Seven Dimensions of the Physical World:** These correspond to the seven base quantities and are denoted using square brackets `[ ]`.
    - Length: **[L]**
    - Mass: **[M]**
    - Time: **[T]**
    - Electric Current: **[A]**
    - Thermodynamic Temperature: **[K]**
    - Luminous Intensity: **[cd]**
    - Amount of Substance: **[mol]**
- **Definition of Dimensions:** The dimensions of a physical quantity are the **powers (exponents)** to which the base quantities are raised to represent that quantity.
    - *Example: Volume*
        - Volume = length $\times$ breadth $\times$ height = length $\times$ length $\times$ length
        - Dimensions of Volume = $[L] \times [L] \times [L] = [L^3]$
        - This means volume has three dimensions in length and zero dimensions in mass and time ($[M^0 L^3 T^0]$).
    - *Example: Force*
        - Force = mass $\times$ acceleration
        - Acceleration = length / $(time)^2$
        - Dimensions of Force = $[M] \times ([L] / [T]^2) = [M L T^{-2}]$
        - This means force has one dimension in mass, one in length, and -2 in time.
- **Quality vs. Magnitude:** Dimensional analysis focuses on the *type* or *nature* of the physical quantity, not its specific numerical value or magnitude.
    - *Example:* Change in velocity, initial velocity, final velocity, and speed all have the same dimensions of $[L T^{-1}]$ (length/time).

**Key Takeaway:** Dimensions describe the fundamental composition of a physical quantity using base quantities (L, M, T, A, K, cd, mol) raised to certain powers.
