### 1.2 EVOLUTION OF COMPUTER

- The journey of computing devices has been a fascinating one, from simple counting tools to today's powerful machines, all in a relatively short period of human history.
- **Key Milestones and Inventions**:
    - **Abacus (around 3000 years ago)**: One of the earliest known mechanical devices used for basic arithmetic calculations (addition, subtraction).
    - **Pascaline (1642)**: Invented by the French mathematician **Blaise Pascal**. It was a mechanical calculator capable of performing addition and subtraction directly, and multiplication/division through repeated additions/subtractions.
    - **Analytic Engine (1834)**: Designed by **Charles Babbage**, often called the "Father of the Computer." This was a revolutionary mechanical general-purpose computer that included concepts like input, processing, storage, and output, forming the theoretical basis of modern computers.
    - **Tabulating Machine (1890)**: Developed by **Herman Hollerith** to quickly process census data in the US. It read information from **punched cards** (stiff paper with holes representing data). This machine was a precursor to programming and data processing.
    - **EDVAC/ENIAC (1945)**: **John Von Neumann** introduced the crucial concept of the **stored program computer**, meaning both data and instructions (programs) could be stored in the computer's memory. **ENIAC** (Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer) was one of the first electronic general-purpose binary programmable computers, based on the Von Neumann architecture.
    - **Transistor (1947)**: Invented at Bell Labs. This tiny electronic switch replaced bulky and heat-generating **vacuum tubes**, leading to much smaller, faster, and more reliable computers.
    - **Integrated Circuit (IC) (1970)**: Also known as a **microchip**, an IC is a tiny silicon chip containing many electronic components (like transistors, resistors) in a single compact package. This invention further miniaturized computers dramatically.
    - **Moore's Law (1965)**: Co-founder of Intel, **Gordon Moore**, observed that the number of transistors on a microchip would approximately **double every two years**, while the cost for the same processing power would be halved. This prediction has largely held true, driving rapid technological advancement.
        - **Formula for transistor count (approximate)**: $N(t) = N_0 \times 2^{t/2}$ where $N(t)$ is the number of transistors at time $t$ (in years), and $N_0$ is the initial number.
    - **Microprocessor (1970s)**: Advancements like **Large Scale Integration (LSI)** allowed entire CPUs to be manufactured on a single IC chip.
    - **Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) (1980s)**: Enabled the integration of millions of components (around 3 million) onto a single chip, leading to exponential increases in computing power.
    - **Super Large Scale Integration (SLSI)**: Further refined VLSI, allowing approximately $10^6$ components to be fabricated on a single IC.
    - **Personal Computers (PCs)**:
        - IBM launched its first PC in 1981, bringing computers into homes.
        - Apple introduced its Macintosh computers in 1984, popularizing user-friendly interfaces.
        - The widespread adoption of **Graphical User Interface (GUI)**-based operating systems (like Microsoft Windows) made computers much easier to use than older **command-line interfaces** (like UNIX or DOS).
    - **World Wide Web (WWW) (1990s)**: The internet, particularly the World Wide Web, transformed computers into essential tools for communication, information, and commerce, integrating them into daily life.
    - **Portability**: Miniaturization of processors and advances in memory and high-speed connectivity led to the development of portable devices like laptops, smartphones, tablets, and personal digital assistants (PDAs).
    - **Future of Computing**: The trend continues towards even smaller, more integrated devices, including **wearable gadgets** (smartwatches, smart lenses) and smart home appliances, all connected in the **Internet of Things (IoT)**, often powered by **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**.
