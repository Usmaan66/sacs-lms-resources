### 1.5.3 Law of Multiple Proportions

-   Proposed by **Dalton** in 1803.
-   **Statement**: If two elements can combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element are in the ratio of small whole numbers.
-   **Example**: Hydrogen and oxygen combine to form water ($H_2O$) and hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$).
    -   **Water**: 2g Hydrogen + 16g Oxygen $\rightarrow$ 18g Water
    -   **Hydrogen Peroxide**: 2g Hydrogen + 32g Oxygen $\rightarrow$ 34g Hydrogen Peroxide
    -   Here, with a fixed mass of hydrogen (2g), the masses of oxygen (16g and 32g) are in a simple ratio of $16:32$, or $1:2$.
