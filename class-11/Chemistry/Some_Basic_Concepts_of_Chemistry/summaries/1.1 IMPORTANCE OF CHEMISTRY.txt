## 1.1 IMPORTANCE OF CHEMISTRY

-   Chemistry is a central science, interconnected with other scientific branches.
-   Its principles apply to diverse areas:
    -   Weather patterns
    -   Brain functioning
    -   Computer operation
    -   Chemical industries (fertilizers, alkalis, acids, salts, dyes, polymers, drugs, soaps, detergents, metals, alloys, new materials)
-   **Contributions to National Economy**: Large-scale production of fertilizers, pesticides, and insecticides.
-   **Healthcare**: Provides methods for isolating and synthesizing life-saving drugs.
    -   Examples: **Cisplatin** and **Taxol** (cancer therapy), **AZT** (Azidothymidine) for AIDS patients.
-   **Material Science**: Understanding chemical principles allows designing and synthesizing new materials with specific magnetic, electric, and optical properties.
    -   Examples: **Superconducting ceramics**, **conducting polymers**, **optical fibers**.
-   **Industries**: Helps establish industries manufacturing utility goods like acids, alkalis, dyes, polymers, metals, contributing to the economy and employment.
-   **Environmental Impact**: Helped in creating safer alternatives for hazardous substances like **CFCs** (chlorofluorocarbons), which cause ozone depletion.
-   **Future Challenges**: Chemists face challenges in managing greenhouse gases (methane, carbon dioxide), understanding biochemical processes, using enzymes for chemical production, and synthesizing exotic materials.
