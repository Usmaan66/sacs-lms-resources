#### Vegetative Propagation (in Plants)

- In plants, asexual reproduction is often referred to as **vegetative propagation**.
- Specialized structures are involved in vegetative propagation, and these are called **vegetative propagules**.

| Vegetative Propagule | Plant Example |
| :------------------- | :------------ |
| Runner               | Grass, Oxalis |
| Rhizome              | Ginger, Turmeric, Banana |
| Sucker               | Mint, Chrysanthemum |
| Tuber                | Potato        |
| Offset               | Water hyacinth (Eichhornia), Pistia |
| Bulb                 | Onion         |
| Bulbil               | Agave         |
| Leaf Buds            | Bryophyllum   |

- **Eyes (Potato Tuber)**: The small indentations on potato tubers are axillary buds, also called "eyes." Each eye can give rise to a new plant.
- **Rhizome (Ginger)**: Underground stems with nodes and internodes that can produce new shoots.
- **Bulbil (Agave)**: Fleshy buds that develop in the axil of leaves and can form new plants.
- **Leaf Buds (Bryophyllum)**: Adventitious buds arising from the notches along the margins of leaves that can develop into new plantlets.
- **Water Hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes)**: An invasive aquatic weed, also known as "Terror of Bengal," due to its rapid vegetative propagation through **offset** and its ability to deoxygenate water bodies, leading to the death of aquatic life.
