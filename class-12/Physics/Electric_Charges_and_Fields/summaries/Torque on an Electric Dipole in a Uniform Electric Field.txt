### Torque on an Electric Dipole in a Uniform Electric Field

-   When an electric dipole is placed in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$, the two charges experience equal and opposite forces, forming a **couple**.
-   This couple exerts a **torque ($\vec{\tau}$)** on the dipole, tending to align it with the electric field.
-   Formula for torque:
    $$\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}$$
-   Magnitude of torque: $\tau = pE \sin\theta$
    -   Where $\theta$ is the angle between the dipole moment $\vec{p}$ and the electric field $\vec{E}$.
-   **Direction:** The direction of torque is perpendicular to the plane containing $\vec{p}$ and $\vec{E}$, given by the right-hand rule.
-   **Special Cases:**
    -   If $\theta = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ (dipole aligned with the field), $\tau = 0$.
    -   If $\theta = 90^\circ$ (dipole perpendicular to the field), $\tau = pE$ (maximum torque).
