### Electric Flux Definition

-   **Electric flux ($\Phi_E$)** is a measure of the number of electric field lines passing normally through a given area.
-   It is a scalar quantity.
-   For a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ passing through a plane area $\vec{A}$ (where $\vec{A}$ is a vector normal to the surface, with magnitude equal to the area):
    $$\Phi_E = \vec{E} \cdot \vec{A} = EA \cos\theta$$
    -   Where $\theta$ is the angle between the electric field vector $\vec{E}$ and the area vector $\vec{A}$.
-   If the electric field is non-uniform or the surface is not flat, the flux is calculated by integrating over the surface:
    $$\Phi_E = \int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A}$$
-   The SI unit of electric flux is **newton meter squared per coulomb (N m$^2$/C)** or **volt meter (V m)**.
