## Majoritarianism in Sri Lanka

- The Sri Lankan government's policies led to a civil war, a violent conflict within a country.
- In **1956**, an Act was passed to make Sinhala the only official language, disregarding Tamil.
- The government followed **preferential policies** that favored Sinhala applicants for university positions and government jobs.
- A new constitution stipulated that the state shall **protect and foster Buddhism**.
- These measures made the Sri Lankan Tamils feel alienated, discriminated against, and ignored.
- They launched parties demanding recognition of Tamil as an official language, regional autonomy, and equal opportunities.
- Their demands were repeatedly denied, leading to distrust and conflict.
- By the 1980s, several political organizations were formed demanding an independent Tamil state in northern and eastern Sri Lanka.
- The conflict turned into a civil war, which caused a terrible setback to the social, cultural, and economic life of the country and ended in 2009.

**Key Takeaway**: Majoritarianism can undermine the unity of a nation and lead to civil war by alienating minority communities.
